Task 2b: Language Focus — Present Participle Clauses

Usage:
A present participle clause has an adverbial meaning, expressing the result of an action.
Form:
Begins with a present participle (e.g., 'resulting in', 'guaranteeing', 'ensuring'). Typically, the subject of the clause is identical to that of the main clause.
用法：现在分词子句具有副词意义，表达行动的结果。形式：以现在分词开头（如 resulting in、guaranteeing、ensuring）。通常子句的主语与主句的主语相同。

Task 1a: Addressing Concerns & Proposing Solutions

任务1a：处理关注事项及提出解决方案

When submitting funding applications, addressing potential concerns proactively demonstrates thorough risk assessment and builds confidence in your project's success.

提交资助申请时，主动处理潜在关注事项展示了全面的风险评估，并建立对其项目成功的信心。

Sample: Supplementary Information

"We understand that there might be concerns about the tight production schedule. However, we will spare no effort in ensuring meticulous planning and timely execution, guaranteeing that the film will be produced on schedule and meet all required standards."

「我们理解外界可能对紧张的制作时间表有所顾虑。我们将不遗余力地确保周密的规划及及时的执行，保证电影能按时制作并达到所有要求的标准。」

Analysis

What expressions are used to introduce the possible concern and solution?

  • Introducing the possible concern: "We understand that there might be concerns about..."
  • Introducing the solution: "However, we will spare no effort in..."

What effect does the phrase 'there might be concerns about' have on the tone?

This phrase introduces a potential issue in a tentative and polite manner. The word 'might' implies uncertainty, presenting the concern as a possibility rather than a confirmed problem. It softens the tone, avoiding sounding alarming.

What effect does the phrase 'we will spare no effort in...' have on the tone?

This phrase creates a tone of strong dedication and promise. The words 'will' and 'spare no effort' show firm commitment, making the message sound confident and certain.

Which word facilitates the transition from potential concern to reassurance?

The word 'However' facilitates the transition. It contrasts the potential concern with the proactive response, guiding the reader from doubt to confidence.

Language Focus: Modals & Modifiers for Possibility and Certainty

Expressing Tentativeness (Possibility) Conveying Confidence & Commitment (Certainty)
Modals: can, could, may, might
Modifiers: perhaps, probably, potentially, possibly, maybe, sometimes
Patterns:
— We understand/are aware that there might be concerns about...
— It is likely that certain issues could arise...
— Possible drawbacks might involve... 		Modals: will
Adverbs: absolutely, undoubtedly, definitely, certainly, clearly
Patterns:
— We will spare no effort in (+ gerund)
— We will commit fully to (+ gerund)
— We will take every measure to ensure that (+ clause)
— Please rest assured that...
表达不确定性（可能性）：can, could, may, might; perhaps, probably, potentially, possibly — 我们明白/意识到可能会有……的顾虑；某些问题可能会出现……

LAN4103 Supplementary Notes for CA

Follow the 4 steps suggested to write your 25% CA - Prepared by Nancy and Rebecca

🔑 How to read the colour-coding & symbols in this guide

Each Step below has its own numbered/lettered set of sentence structures (e.g. Structure 1, Structure 2…). The SAME highlight colour is used for a structure box and for the matching phrase in every example that follows it, in this fixed order — so you can trace a colour straight from the formula to the real sentence:

1st   2nd   3rd   4th   5th   ← order of structures/parts in each Step

On top of the colours, four more marks show four different language skills:

• Bold = fixed “skeleton” wording — copy this part exactly as it is.

• Italics = a bank of interchangeable words/phrases — pick ONE option that fits your case.

• Bold + underline = a linking/transition or sequencing word (e.g. Additionally, However, In phase 1,) — these connect your ideas and can be reused in any similar report.

• Plain (highlighted but not bold/italic/underlined) = the specific content you write yourself for your own project (names, numbers, benefits, concerns, etc.).

Tip: cover the examples and try to rebuild each sentence using only the colour + bold/italic/underline cues in the structure box — that is the fastest way to revise.

Step 1: Writing Organisation Information (Unit 2)

For writing Organisation information (Unit 2), identify key components on the homepage and use the following grammatical structures:

• Structure 1: [past participle, "-ed"] + in + Establishment/Founding/Formation year

• Structure 2: Organization name + is a + [business nature]

• Structure 3: specialising in / dedicated to / committed to / devoted to / focusing on + [action / area]

• Structure 4: Additionally, / Also, + we organize / run / conduct / provide / offer (v.) + organization's mission / services / work

• Structure 5: [to + verb] + purpose of the mission / services / work

Read the following examples:

Example 1 (InnoDesign): Formed in 2001, InnoDesign is a product design consultancy devoted to offering customised product prototyping services to local and international manufacturers. Also, we host community workshops to engage participants in hands-on product design activities.

Example 2 (StyleScope): Started in 2020, StyleScope Hong Kong is a fashion media company focusing on publishing fashion and beauty magazines for style enthusiasts. Additionally, we run reader engagement campaigns to invite fashion ideas or styling tips from readers.

Example 3 (Island Esports): Established/Founded in 2013, Island Esports is a gaming association dedicated to nurturing esports talent in Hong Kong. Also, we offer a range of esport games and competitions to nurture positive gaming culture.

Step 2: Writing Project Details (Implementation + Amount of Funding Requested) (Unit 3)

For writing Project details (Implementation + Amount of funding requested) (Unit 3), identify duration and action of each phase on Notes of meeting. Use the following vocabulary and grammatical structures:

a. Implementation Formula:
• The project will be completed in three stages/phases, each lasting XXX months/weeks.
• In phase 1, we will [verb]..., in phase 2, we will [verb]...  OR
• In phase 1, ... will be [+ p.p.]. In phase 2, ... will be [+ p.p.]
• In phase 3, / In the final phase, ...

b. Amount of Funding Requested Formula:
• It is estimated that a total of $xxxxx will be required for the project. For details of the breakdown of the costs, please refer to the attachment.
• (Alternative) The project is estimated to cost around $xxxxx. The details of the breakdown are attached for your information.

Read the following examples:

Example 1 — Film / Drama Project:
a. Implementation:
The project will be completed in three phases, each lasting two months. In phase 1, we will finalise the screenplay and cast suitable actors. In phase 2, necessary filming permits will be obtained before scheduling rehearsals. Filming will also be conducted to capture the scripted scenes. In the final phase, we will edit the footage and test the submission entry on multiple devices to ensure technical compatibility.

b. Amount of funding requested:
It is estimated that a total of $1,000,000 will be required for the project. For details of the breakdown, please refer to the attachment.

Example 2 — Neon Art Exhibition:
a. Implementation:
The project will be executed in three stages, each spanning three weeks. In stage 1, we will send invitations to local neon artists and co-create artworks with them. In stage 2, the exhibition will be installed at a public venue. Interactive workshops will also be incorporated. In the final stage, we will conduct random interviews with visitors and collect their feedback to evaluate the project's impact for future development.

b. Amount of funding requested:
The project is estimated to cost around $400,000. The details of the breakdown are attached for your information.

Step 3: Writing Justification for Funding (Unit 4)

For writing Justification (Unit 4), identify 3 benefits on Your handwritten notes. Use the following vocabulary and grammatical structures:

1. Benefit 1: The project will + provide / offer / feature / give (v.) + [Benefit 1].

2. Elaboration on Benefit 1: verb+ing (e.g. strengthening / encouraging) + [result / elaboration on Benefit 1]

3. Benefit 2: Additionally, / Moreover, / In addition, it / the project / participants will + [Benefit 2],

4. Elaboration on Benefit 2: which + [result / elaboration on Benefit 2]

5. Alignment with Funder: Furthermore, / In addition, / Also, this project's aim shares/ closely aligns with / is in line with the mission and values of [funder]. It will... (Tip: Avoid repeating "will" — use: contributing to, resulting in, leading to, enabling, fostering + verb-ing...)

Read the following examples:

Example 1 (Creative Industry):
The competition will provide a platform for our emerging creators to showcase their work, strengthening the industry's creative pipeline. In addition, the funding will enable us to secure essential production resources, which helps to ensure high-quality post-production work. Furthermore, the aim of the project closely aligns with the mission of the Cultural and Creative Industries Development Agency. It will contribute greatly to the sustainable growth of Hong Kong's cultural and creative industries.

Example 2 (Neon Art & Heritage):
The neon exhibition will highlight the craftsmanship and cultural significance of neon art, encouraging the public to rediscover this unique visual heritage. Local artists will also have a platform to experiment with light-based media, which will foster creative exchange. Moreover, the project's aim is in line with the mission and values of the CreateSmart Initiative, resulting in promoting design appreciation and cultural preservation in the community.

Step 4: Writing Supplementary Information (Concerns, Solutions & Conclusion) (Unit 5)

For writing Supplementary information (Unit 5), identify the possible concern and solution and a concluding remark on Your handwritten notes. Then, identify the project aim on Notes of meeting to write a concluding remark. Use the following vocabulary and grammatical structures:

1. Acknowledging Concern: We are aware that / We acknowledge / We understand that there might be concerns about ... [concern]

2. Proposing Solution: However, we will ensure that... / However, please rest assured that we will... [solution]

3. Concluding Remark Formula: We are confident that the project / [verb-ing phrase], which [project aim], will [summary of benefits / positive outcome].

Read the following examples:

Example 1 — Esports Tournament (Technical Server Lag):
We are aware that there might be concerns about technical issues, such as server lag during the tournament. However, please rest assured that we will secure high-performance servers and conduct pre-event stress tests so that a smooth experience for all participants will be guaranteed.

We are confident that organising the esports tournament, which showcases the outstanding performance of local gamers, will foster a positive perception of competitive gaming among the public, gaining more support from society.

Example 2 — EV Charging Station Network (Equipment Safety):
We understand that there might be concerns about the safety of the charging stations. However, we will ensure regular check-ups and maintenance of the charging equipment so that the risk of malfunctions can be minimized.

We are confident that installing the EV charging station network, which promotes sustainable transportation and a greener future, will have a significant and far-reaching impact on environmental conservation, promoting the adoption of EVs in the community.

Example 3 — Neon Installation Exhibition (Electrical Safety):
We acknowledge that there might be concerns about the safety of neon installations. However, we are committed to ensuring that all artworks comply with electrical safety standards and undergo professional inspection before being displayed in order to minimise risks.

We are confident that the neon exhibition, which supports local creative talent, will generate meaningful cultural impact and inspire interest in preserving the city's design legacy.
